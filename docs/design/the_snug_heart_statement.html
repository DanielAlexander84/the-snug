The Snug · A co-working tavern

Why this exists

A place to begin the things you’ve been avoiding — warm, quiet, alongside others.

For a lot of us, the hardest part of a task isn’t doing it. It’s starting it. “Sort the insurance,” “reply to that email,” “call the doctor” — small things that somehow grow heavy, sit untouched for weeks, and quietly pile up shame.

The Snug is a cosy room you step into when you’re stuck. You name the thing you’re avoiding — plainly, or as vaguely as you like. A gentle guide helps break it into pieces so small the first one is almost too easy. Then you do it, here, next to other people who are doing their own hard small things — and someone sees you finish. That’s the whole magic: you did a real thing, and you weren’t alone.

The heart of it is simple: to help, support, and lift people up.

Not to keep you here. Not to win your attention. To help you begin — and to be glad when, one day, you need us a little less.

What makes it different

  • The reward is real. No points, no levels, no streaks to protect. When you finish something true, a lantern lights and a person nods — because the win lives out in your life, not inside an app.
  • Being seen is the point. Witnessing is warm and light — a quiet “saw that.” Never proof, never surveillance. Just another human noticing you showed up.
  • No shame, ever. Disappear for a month and come back — you’ll be met like a regular walking back into the pub, not scolded for a broken streak.
  • Together, not ranked. Everyone’s small victories weave into one shared cloth. Not “look what I did,” but “look what we can all do when we put our hearts in.”

Who it’s for

People — many of us with ADHD — who are tired of being told to just try harder, who’ve been burned by apps that promised to fix them and added pressure instead. People who don’t need another productivity tool. They need a warm corner, a gentle hand to start, and someone to notice they did.

The vows

Every screen, every word, every choice gets checked against these first.

  • Does a tired, ashamed, overwhelmed person feel lifted and met — or judged?
  • Is the reward a real thing in the real world, or a shiny thing in here?
  • Does this welcome someone back without guilt?
  • Does it say “we” — never “you, compared to them”?
  • Would we be glad if someone needed us less because of it?
